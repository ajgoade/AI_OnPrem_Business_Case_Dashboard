    Methodology & Assumptions ▸

    Token demand

    Base monthly tokens = AIC used/month × average tokens per AI Credit. Uncounted tokens (code completion, intermediate agentic steps not captured by AIC billing) are added on top of this base only for HPE AI Hybrid's GPU load — cloud/AIC spend is unaffected, since it bills on counted credits.

    HPE AI Hybrid GPU throughput

    Tok/s per GPU for each model in the mix is estimated with a power-law fit calibrated to two reference points (~10,000 tok/s for an 8B dense model, ~2,500 tok/s for a 70B dense model on H200 at FP8): tok/s ≈ 43,600 × active_params(B)-0.639 × type multiplier, where the multiplier is 1.0 for dense models, 0.85 for verbose reasoning/distilled models, and 0.6 for Mixture-of-Experts models (applied to active, not total, parameters). These are directional engineering estimates, not benchmarked figures — every tok/s value can be overwritten with your own data. The blended tok/s across your model mix is then scaled by a GPU type multiplier: H200 (141GB HBM3e, NVL4) is the 1.00× baseline; RTX PRO 6000 Server Edition (96GB GDDR7, ~1.6TB/s bandwidth vs. H200's ~4.8TB/s, no NVLink) is modeled at 0.40× to reflect its substantially lower memory bandwidth for inference.

    These are aggregate, continuously-batched throughput estimates — not single-user chat speed. Tok/s per GPU here represents total output across many concurrent requests being served together, which is the right lens for sizing organizational capacity. It is not comparable to the "tokens/sec" figure you'd see in a single-chat benchmark or a model provider's latency page, where one user's stream is measured in isolation — that number is typically far lower per request, since aggregate throughput is spread across many simultaneous users, not delivered to any one of them.

    Real throughput will vary from these estimates — often significantly — based on factors this model does not capture at the per-request level: context length (longer prompts consume more time in the compute-bound prefill phase before generation even starts, and MLPerf-style benchmarks that widen input/output length can shift throughput by 2× or more); KV cache pressure (longer conversations and larger context windows consume more GPU memory for cached attention state, which shrinks the batch size the GPU can hold and directly reduces aggregate throughput — this is a big part of why H200's larger HBM over H100 helps so much); batch size and request concurrency (throughput scales up with more simultaneous requests until memory or latency SLAs become the constraint, and production traffic rarely arrives at a steady, ideal batch size); quantization (FP8, FP4, INT8, etc. trade some accuracy for meaningfully higher throughput, and the mix % / tok-s figures here assume you've picked a precision already); and serving stack & tuning (vLLM, TensorRT-LLM, SGLang and similar frameworks, plus how well batching, scheduling, and parallelism are tuned for your specific hardware, can easily produce a 2–3× spread on identical GPUs). Treat every tok/s figure as a planning-grade estimate to validate with a proof-of-concept on your actual model mix and traffic pattern, not a guarantee.

    Capacity

    Theoretical annual capacity = GPU count × blended tok/s × seconds/year. Effective annual capacity applies the useful capacity factor to account for batching inefficiency, request overhead, and headroom. Utilization in the 3-year forecast is demand (including uncounted tokens, compounded by monthly growth) as a percentage of theoretical capacity, compared against a threshold line set at the capacity factor — so raising the capacity factor slider raises how much of theoretical capacity is considered "useful" before more GPUs are needed.

    Cost & TCO

    Annual HPE AI Hybrid TCO = annualized capex + annual support/power/ops, entered directly rather than derived from GPU list pricing (so it reflects your actual quote). Cloud cost compounds the monthly growth rate across 36 months; HPE AI Hybrid TCO is treated as fixed infrastructure cost per year regardless of growth, unless demand exceeds capacity (flagged separately in Key Findings). 3-year TCO for each side is the sum of its 36 monthly costs.

    The support/power/ops figure should include staffing, not just facilities. Real-world HPE AI Hybrid deployments commonly run 0.5–1.5 FTE of infrastructure/MLOps engineering time to keep a GPU cluster healthy — driver and firmware updates, model serving optimization, monitoring, and capacity planning. It's easy to price out power, cooling, and support contracts and forget the people; if that cost isn't already reflected in your annual ops number, add it before treating the TCO comparison as final.

    ROI & payback

    3-Year ROI % = (3-yr cloud TCO − 3-yr HPE AI Hybrid TCO) / 3-yr HPE AI Hybrid TCO. Payback period uses Year-1 cloud spend compounded with monthly growth (not a flat ×12), divided into monthly savings, to show how many months of savings it takes to offset the annual HPE AI Hybrid TCO.

    All figures are directional planning estimates for early-stage business case conversations, not a quote. Validate GPU pricing, capex amortization, support costs, and throughput benchmarks with HPE Financial Services and the NVIDIA/AMD performance teams before presenting externally.
